# Weekly Kumite

Each UTC week has one tournament with **eight human entrants** and a single bracket: four quarterfinals, two semifinals, one final. Registration opens Monday at 00:00 UTC. Seeding occurs at Saturday 00:00 UTC. Quarterfinal, semifinal and final deadlines are Saturday 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC. A known next pairing can play early; these are completion deadlines, not waiting periods.

`GET /api/kumite/current` supplies the public bracket and, for a signed-in player, their registration and next match. `GET /api/kumite/winners` supplies up to 52 actual champion snapshots. Both are `private, no-store`; identities in brackets are opaque entrant IDs, and private user IDs and rival deck cards are excluded.

Each match includes `startedAt`, `completedAt` and `durationMs` from its server-issued battle room. Starting records the moment both assigned humans ready up. Completed battles record the actual command time, and turn timeouts record the server-enforced turn cutoff. A bye or unattended forfeit has no invented battle duration. Draw replays issue a fresh room and show that battle's timing.

Signed-in current views include lifetime `progression` totals and eight roadmap records with `metric`, actual catalog `rarity`, `progress`, `claimed` and `owned`. Bushedo Brown unlocks after one complete six-round Kumite battle, Franklin Duke after the first win from a complete battle, and Chonk Lee after winning a complete-battle final. Dragonfly Jones unlocks after three full wins, Undercova Brotha after five, and Sho'Nuff after eight. Leroy unlocks after two full-final victories and Yasuke after three. Existing identities, abilities, rarities and other acquisition sources remain intact: Dragonfly Jones and Sho'Nuff are Legendary; Undercova Brotha is Rare; Leroy and Yasuke are Mythical. A draw counts as participation, but replaying the same bracket match cannot increment participation again. Registration, byes, no-shows, surrenders and turn timeouts do not earn card progress. A forfeit may legitimately decide the bracket and its historical champion, but it cannot farm the champion card. All grants are automatic: tournament/room/profile locks and permanent unique collection receipts commit the result and owned/discovered card updates together. Totals and ownership persist across weeks. New roadmap steps also reconcile against genuine earlier lifetime receipts when the signed-in current view is fetched; an already-owned card keeps its existing XP and creates only one milestone receipt.

Public current views also supply `records` for the most recent 52 stored weekly tournaments: one fastest decisive full fight with the actual server room timestamps and frozen opponents, up to ten recent full-final winners, and up to ten hand throwers ranked by actual full-fight wins, then full-final victories. These bounded public rankings are separate from each player's lifetime roadmap totals. Records independently validate the issued room, both participant identities, full engine completion, decisive winner and deadline receipt. Surrenders, timeouts, byes, draws and missing/invalid rooms cannot enter these lists. Empty history returns a null fastest fight and empty arrays. The general immutable champion archive remains available through `/winners`, including legitimate bracket champions decided by forfeits.

Historical record reads use at most two bounded SQL selects: at most 52 tournament snapshots, then one batch containing at most 364 compact room proofs. SQL projects only participant IDs used internally for validation, engine completion phase, room status/result, and server timestamp receipts. Hands, decks, boards and presentation logs are never transferred into the record aggregation. The response contains only frozen public fighter presentation and record values. A warm worker keeps one public records snapshot for 15 seconds. Its key includes settled receipt fields and frozen participants, so a new settlement or changed presentation refreshes immediately. Returned snapshots are copied. Current brackets, actual match times and personal progression are never cached. A cold worker still performs the bounded proof batch; SQL projection reduces database-to-server bytes, while PostgreSQL may still read/decompress full stored room JSON internally. Production database throughput and cold-worker frequency have not been benchmarked.

The three fighters are real catalog cards with separate printed abilities and three growth tiers. They are excluded from Street Pack card and variant pools, Neighborhood Recruit and borrowed draft/filler pools. Once earned, they are legal in any ordinary saved gang that passes the owned-card rules. The frontend refreshes player bootstrap after server-reported grants.

`POST /api/kumite/register` takes `{ "deckId": "saved-deck-id" }`. The profile must have completed onboarding, and the same ten unique owned-card/hero validation as online PvP applies. The issued deck and player presentation are frozen at signup. Repeating the same signup is safe and creates one entry. A different deck requires withdrawal first. `POST /api/kumite/withdraw` takes `{}` and is permitted until seeding. The eight-seat capacity check, signup and seeds are serialized across server instances with a PostgreSQL transaction advisory lock.

`POST /api/kumite/match` takes `{}`. It checks the entrant in, issues or reconnects to their assigned room, and returns `{ "room": OnlineRoomView | null, "current": KumiteCurrentView }`. Both assigned humans must ready up using the existing `/api/multiplayer/:code/actions` API. There is no tournament result-submission endpoint. Bracket advancement reads the server's persisted room and validates its tournament ID, match ID, participants, game number and completion timestamp. Turns use the existing 75-second server deadline. Tournament rooms reject rematch votes and late commands. A draw issues a fresh battle with the same locked decks when the entrant next checks in.

At a round cutoff, a completed game or an already-expired 75-second turn settles first. If no game has settled, exactly one checked-in entrant advances by forfeit. If both or neither checked in, both forfeit and the downstream seat stays empty. Two through seven entrants are seeded with distributed byes. Fewer than two entrants cancels the week. A final with no surviving entrant cancels the tournament without inventing a champion. There are no replacement bots and no client-claimed wins.

Deadline reconciliation occurs inside current, winners, register, withdraw and match requests; it needs no continuously running server. The native `kumite-tick` scheduled function additionally runs every 15 minutes in UTC on a published Netlify deploy, so starts, forfeits and archives progress without any player requests. A delayed run still uses the exact saved deadlines. A week viewed after its final cutoff is reconciled and archived before the next week's view is returned. Completed room receipts survive later reads so a late bracket poll cannot erase a valid result. Local development and deploy previews use the API reconciliation path; the published schedule is not active there.

The Drizzle schema is in `lib/db/src/schema/kumite.ts`. Apply the native migration `netlify/database/migrations/202610130001_kumite/migration.sql` as part of an authorized release before exposing the live mode. It installs both tables and a database trigger that rejects updates and deletions of champion history. The archive is an immutable name/hero/avatar snapshot and survives later account presentation changes. Roadmap grants use the existing player collection-claim ledger and need no extra reward migration. Currency, loot and skin grants are not configured.

Run `pnpm run test:campaign:db kumite` for the guarded, owned in-memory database suite. It covers signup/auth/ownership/capacity races, locked deck snapshots, seven-game advancement, reconnection, room access control, immutable history and existing online battle regressions. The pure tests additionally cover UTC boundaries, byes, no-show cancellation, public privacy, draws, server deadlines and participant substitution rejection.
