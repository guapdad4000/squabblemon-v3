01 / A controlled material study

The same Fire print.
Three material recipes.

Keep the red/orange fire illustration recognizable across rarities. Legendary gets selective gold engraving and champagne reflection. Mythical gets crimson detailing and the small circular foil pattern. The broad black center, character area, label, and rules stay visually quiet.

Bake the ink, grain, emboss detail and resting foil pattern. Calculate the moving reflection separately. Baking a bright moving-looking rainbow into every background would lock the glare in place and make cards compete with their own artwork.

02 / Ten coordinated worlds

Elemental print, not scenic wallpaper.

The background family takes its composition from your reference: angled ink, torn edges, controlled halftone, quiet dark portrait space and a common black rules area. Each element changes the motif as well as the color. The illustrations below contain no UI or text.

Final art pass: reduce any excessive saturated area behind the figure; preserve 60–70% quiet space. Light must remain pale, not resemble Legendary gold. Dark and Poison need distinct silhouettes in grayscale. Plant and Earth remain separate types.

The universal lower paper

One shared black ink field gives ability copy a dependable surface. Keep its interior low-noise and let the distressed edge carry the personality. Use fixed-scale end caps and a stretchable quiet center in the final component so the texture and torn corners do not distort for long rules.

Standalone transparent master. The final production export should separate its edge texture from the calm center for flexible rule lengths.

03 / A silhouette, not a chip

Fist first. Element to the right.

The original gold fist stays the visual anchor across all ten elements. The element emerges behind the right knuckles and travels to the right or upper-right. Remove the enclosing rectangular backing, left-side decorations, and nested outlines. At thumbnail size, the fist and elemental tail must still read as two deliberate shapes.

Shape contract

Use one approved fist drawing as the master. Keep the original gold color, knuckle rhythm, thumb position, wrist cut and visual weight fixed. Fist occupies roughly 55–60% of width; the elemental extension occupies the rest. Allow a slight upward tail, never an enclosure.

Placement contract

In collection and full details, sit the emblem at the lower-right of the art, alongside the label when space permits. Long names may claim more width; then lift the emblem into the art margin instead of squeezing the name. It never sits over rules or statistics.

Cleanup before use

The refined sheet preserves the gold fist and removes the earlier broad glow. Final assets still need exact shared fist geometry, equal visual weight, consistent gutters, and clean alpha edges. Differentiate Normal’s impact burst from Electric’s lightning at 24 px. Export individual marks with a simplified small-size version; do not ship a cropped concept atlas.

Target display checks: 24 / 32 / 48 / 72 CSS px. At the smallest size, remove tiny debris and halftone. Retain the accessible element name even where only the icon is visible. No crowns or unrelated symbols.

05 / The compositing contract

Make the layers explicit.

  1. 08Live UI and interactionsMotion, Hands, status, focus, accessible text. Always authoritative.
  2. 07Postal name + rarity stampBlzee title; small source-derived rarity label. Opaque paper.
  3. 06Rules paper + real rule textAdaptive-height black field. No foil over copy.
  4. 05Element fist + approved foreground accentsTransparent open silhouette, positioned around name width.
  5. 04Existing character artOriginal proportions and intentional backdrop. Protected from full-card color grading.
  6. 03Selective foil reflectionOptional GPU/CSS light using the same allowed mask.
  7. 02Rarity print / engravingBaked rest appearance, roughness and foil-mask definition.
  8. 01Element ink + quiet substrateTen coordinated printed backgrounds, common registration.

Shared canvas

Retain the existing 63:88 collectible ratio. Author masters at 1260 × 1760. Use normalized coordinates so the same safe areas apply at every size. The reference's decorative edge may overhang; hit targets and layout dimensions stay rectangular and stable.

Starting composition

Keep the top 10% available for Motion, Hands and the wordmark. Character art uses the central region with object-fit: contain. The name/emblem row sits near the lower art edge, roughly 63–77% in a full-detail composition. The rules region begins near 77%, with flexible height.

These are proposed art guides, not a fixed clipping rule. Collection thumbnails, full rules cards and unusually long text need different text budgets. Do not shrink or crop a character to force every asset into one baked box.

Protect the readable surfaces

Use explicit foil masks for perimeter ink and background islands. Exclude stats, character alpha, postal paper, rules paper and focus indicators. The static fallback and live shader consume the same mask. No overall hue filter or saturation wash on the finished card.

06 / Keep the label, fix its fit

The paper follows the lettering.

The label is close. GUAP and KYLE need less empty paper at the right; FOLKS and BUDDY are useful approved anchors. Measuring the loaded Blzee glyphs gives a better fit than applying one minimum width to every short name.

Implemented in this audition

The preview now measures short names after Blzee loads. It trims optically narrow names toward a 39–52% card-width minimum while keeping FOLKS/BUDDY at the approved 52% minimum. Long-name shape, typeface and angle remain the current baseline. The rarity caption now uses “Super Rare” for Epic.

Final label geometry

Use fixed-size left/right paper ends with a flexible center. Keep the blue/red print, rough corners and stripe thickness stable. Match side breathing room to glyph ink bounds. Long names wrap deliberately; never stretch the lettering or truncate identity.

Check Oz, GUAP, KYLE, FOLKS, BUDDY, All Jokes Roaster, Racially Ambiguous Transplant and The Guy She Told You Not to Worry About. Reserve space for the longest rarity stamp. Recompute when the font or locale changes.

08 / Build sequence and gates

Prove the system before the batch.

01

Review the assembled Fire pilot

Assemble FOLKS and GUAP using their existing art, the Fire plate, open fist, fitted label and adaptive rules paper. Compare matte, Legendary and Mythical side by side. Add a long-name card and one full-background portrait. Verify each layer independently.

  • Gate: labels fit, art is intact, Fire remains recognizable across rarity, no foil touches readable surfaces.
  • Keep a comparison switch to the current card. Scope: collection, deck builder, full details.
02

Build the full asset family

Produce ten registered element plates and ten independent transparent fist marks from the approved direction. Use the same silhouette master, edge weights and quiet-region template. Create seven reusable rarity pattern/mask recipes and the shared rules-paper pieces.

  • Inventory: 10 plates, 10 marks, 1 shared rules set, 1 postal label system, 7 stock/foil recipes, 5 edition policies.
  • Do not generate 70 independent finished cards. Compose the 10 × 7 combinations from shared art and material rules.
  • Export individual assets; keep these concept sheets in review artifacts only.
03

Implement the resolver and safe surfaces

Move the approved recipe into explicit components and one typed registry. Derive rarity labels from the engine definitions. Add source-data checks for all ten elements and seven tiers. Keep a production feature switch until the full matrix is visually reviewed.

  • Build all 70 element/rarity combinations in an isolated matrix even where the current catalog has zero cards.
  • Exercise all five cosmetic variants on the Fire pilot plus high-contrast Light/Dark cases.
  • Cover portrait, support, Blockbuster, immersive and token/boss exclusions.
04

Budget the graphics and interaction

Grids use cached static finishes. Only a focused or inspected card needs live foil. Reuse the production visibility, reduced-motion and GPU-quality controls; do not import the audition's three-slot renderer as the app-wide solution.

  • Proposed transfer targets: element master ≤180 KB at 756 × 1056; thumbnail ≤30 KB at 252 × 352; transparent emblem ≤18 KB around 384 × 256; compact mask ≤12 KB. These are targets, not measured results for today's PNG studies.
  • Load only visible cards' art. Defer detailed assets and renderer code until inspection. Share one renderer; cap live effects to the active focus/inspector and pause hidden/offscreen work.
  • Reduced motion and GPU failure retain the same still appearance. No continuous idle render loop in a collection grid.
05

Verify, then integrate

Verify actual collection → deck builder → full details → dismiss/return flows, font loading, touch and keyboard, and all card special cases. Keep an asset manifest with hashes and a reversible feature switch. Stage only this feature's tested files when an actual release is requested.

  • Viewport set: 320, 390, 430, 768, 1024, 1280 CSS px, plus short landscape. Check real card widths inside each container.
  • Stress: long names/rules, 3-digit stats, buffed/debuffed Hands, status icons, selected/focused state, 100-card scrolling, cold caches and failed assets.
  • Check 4.5:1 normal-text contrast and 3:1 qualifying large text across the full light sweep, not just a resting screenshot.
  • Measure scroll/frame cost against the current baseline. No performance or physical-device claim until tested.

Old element mapping

getCardWallpaper still lists Ice, Psychic and Fighting. Current Poison, Light and Earth fall through to gold-plaza. The new registry needs all ten actual types plus the Rock → Earth alias.

Competing visual layers

CardView combines wallpaper, atmosphere, rarity patterns, art, shade, engraving, rims, CSS foil, glare, GPU foil and variant sheen. The new contract should consolidate their responsibility, so imported finishes cannot double the visible material.

Legacy crowns

foil-crown.svg contains a crown shape, and the GPU stock-map function draws crown motifs for high tiers/tagged editions. Replace both with fist engraving as part of the approved material implementation.

Wrong label in the audition

The isolated postal label said “EPIC” while the engine's player-facing label is “Super Rare.” This preview wording is corrected. Final labels should read directly from the engine's rarity definitions.

Incomplete finish mapping

The audition maps Rare and Epic to the same crystal effect. Production already distinguishes all seven tiers. Reuse the successful materials as ingredients and complete the hierarchy instead of flattening it.

Renderer scope

The isolated imported renderer has three atlas slots. Production already shares foil tiles and pauses offscreen work. Integrate the approved patterns into the production renderer rather than running both systems.
