## One-line pitch

The man losing an argument with a vending machine is the first to notice the Open sign has been covered.

## Public persona and voice

Escalates three missing dollars into a press conference with a dead microphone. Volunteers himself as investigator, witness, and catering. His confidence increases as the stakes get smaller. He is still the first to point out that Blue covered up the OPEN sign.

## Private truth

He wants people to value his contribution without having to laugh first. He sees practical inequity before he has the vocabulary for it.

**Wants:** A seat at the table and credit for helping set it up.

**Needs:** To learn he can matter without turning every silence into a performance.

## Relationships

Blue underestimates him. Wifey knows he helps. Alley Runner treats his small problem as solvable. The player receives his support before earning any status.

## Season arc

1: Defends the Open's literal promise, loses his drink, and helps at the ceremony. Optional alley: learns the flap was the problem. 3–7: Use sparingly in practical event roles, not mandatory jokes in every serious scene. 8: Has his own assigned setup responsibility and fulfills it.

## Voice samples

- “This machine stole my three dollars. I am outside its place of business demanding accountability.”
- “The sign says OPEN, Blue. You can't put a jacket over a vowel and start a private school.”
- “He said it eleven times in the car. We missed the exit because he wanted bass under it.”
- “First day outside and already somebody's supervisor. That's nasty work.”

## Continuity guardrails
