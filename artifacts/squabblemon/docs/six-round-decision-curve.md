# Six-round decision curve

The shared engine uses this curve for Tutorial, Training, story, and reward-verified matches. Modes may change stakes or apply explicit story modifiers, but they do not replace these decisions.

| Round | Motion target | Understandable decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | Establish a district engine, take a district bonus, or hold a card to conceal the plan. Every starter hand has at least two playable cards. |
| 2 | 2, plus 1 carried | Reinforce the opening claim or pivot toward a second district. Plug and cheap cards begin creating sequencing choices. |
| 3 | 3, plus 1 carried | Contest the rival's telegraphed lane, abandon it for two-lane pressure, or use the first credible SQUABBLE swing. |
| 4 | 4, plus 1 carried | Conditional disruption and comeback abilities become affordable. Players should compare immediate Power with setup value. |
| 5 | 5, plus 1 carried | High-cost closers compete with flexible cheap plays. Saving SQUABBLE now risks losing its value, while using it exposes the final board. |
| 6 | 6 maximum | Convert setup into two district claims. There is no future value in passing or holding SQUABBLE, but lane choice and ability targets remain consequential. |

## Guardrails

- Only one unspent Motion carries into the next round, and total Motion is capped at 6. Passing creates tempo without producing an automatic late turn.
- District bonuses are large enough to alter card value: +2 in The Town and Group Chat, +3 in Server Room.
- Rival intent names a style and likely pressure point, never an exact card.
- SQUABBLE doubles base Power once per match. It is best used to steal a close district or demand a response, not as a hidden end-of-match multiplier.
- Base abilities remain complete at level 1. Sequential upgrades add small, visible Power consequences only after the printed ability succeeds.
- Round summaries report changed ownership and all three live scores. The final scoreboard remains visible before rewards and offers both replay and crew adjustment.
