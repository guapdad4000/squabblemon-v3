# Kumite — weekly tournament guide

Patch 1.13 · prepared October 13, 2026

Kumite is a weekly PvP tournament for up to eight human players. You enter one gang, keep that lineup for the whole bracket, and fight assigned opponents through quarterfinals, semifinals and a final. Full battles also build permanent progress toward eight fighter unlocks.

These release notes are prepared for the private Patch Desk. They have not been published to Events or sent through Mailman. This draft carries **0 Clout and 0 Pack Tickets**.

## Enter this week's tournament

1. Open **Kumite** from Fade Park's PvP modes, or tap the **KUMITE** chibi fight below the Mythic shortcut on the Safehouse.
2. Enter the hallway and choose a battle-ready gang in **Sign up**.
3. Register before the tournament starts. There are eight seats in this week's single bracket.
4. When your pairing is available, tap **Check in & fight**. Both assigned opponents must tap **Ready** in the room to begin.
5. Finish before the displayed round deadline. After a win, return to Kumite and check the bracket for your next opponent.

You must be signed in, have completed onboarding, and own every card in your gang. The gang needs **ten distinct cards**, with a selected hero among those ten. An eligible saved gang or an owned starter recipe can enter; a borrowed draft is not a tournament entry.

Registration locks your card list, hero, gang name and player presentation for this tournament. Editing or deleting the saved gang afterwards does not replace the registered lineup. To switch before the draw, withdraw and register again while registration is open and a seat is available. Withdrawing releases your seat; it does not reserve it for your next signup.

## Weekly schedule

The schedule is fixed in UTC. Kumite displays the actual dates in your browser's local time zone.

| Event | Weekly UTC time |
| --- | --- |
| Registration opens | Monday, 00:00 |
| Registration closes and seeds are drawn | Saturday, 00:00 |
| Quarterfinal completion deadline | Saturday, 06:00 |
| Semifinal completion deadline | Saturday, 12:00 |
| Final completion deadline | Saturday, 18:00 |

The six-hour spacing is a set of **completion deadlines**, not a fresh six-hour clock that starts when you check in. If both feeder matches have finished, the next pairing can play immediately. You do not need to wait for the previous round's cutoff. Conversely, entering a fight close to its deadline leaves only the time remaining.

For the week of October 12, 2026, Los Angeles is on PDT: registration opens Sunday October 11 at 5 PM; the draw is Friday October 16 at 5 PM; quarterfinals close Friday at 11 PM; semifinals and the final close Saturday October 17 at 5 AM and 11 AM. Use the dates shown in your own Kumite screen for subsequent weeks and daylight-saving changes.

## The draw, smaller fields and elimination

A full bracket contains four quarterfinals, two semifinals and one final. Seeds are randomized when the tournament starts. Winning advances you; losing eliminates you from that week's bracket. There is no loser bracket or tournament rematch vote.

The eight-player number is the capacity, rather than a requirement that every seat must fill. With **two to seven entrants**, empty seats produce distributed byes. A bye advances the surviving entrant without a battle. For example, with two entrants the final can become available immediately after byes propagate. Fewer than two entrants cancels the week. If nobody survives to win the final, the tournament ends without inventing a champion. Empty seats are never filled by replacement bots.

## Check-in, readiness and returning to a fight

Opening **Check in & fight** records that you arrived for the pairing and takes you to the assigned room. Check-in and readiness are separate: **both players must press Ready** before the battle starts. A direct reconnect that readies an assigned player also records their check-in.

**Return to your fight** reconnects to the existing room. Leaving the waiting screen through its return control takes you back to Kumite and does not surrender the pairing. The explicit, confirmed forfeit control does surrender it, including before the battle starts; your opponent can advance. A surrender does not earn fighter progress.

The game retains the normal **75-second turn deadline**. Do not leave an active turn waiting: when its valid turn deadline expires, the server settles the loss. Kumite also enforces the pairing's round cutoff, so a late start cannot extend the tournament deadline.

## What happens at a cutoff

A result finished by the cutoff is settled first, even if you only reopen the bracket later. An already-expired turn is also resolved before the no-show rule when its turn deadline falls at or before the round cutoff.

For an unfinished pairing at the cutoff:

| Check-in state | Bracket result |
| --- | --- |
| Exactly one player checked in | That player advances by forfeit |
| Both players checked in, but no game settled | Both forfeit; the next seat is empty |
| Neither player checked in | Both forfeit; the next seat is empty |

Checking in does not guarantee advancement if both players arrive but fail to finish. Finish the assigned fight within the posted deadline. A legitimate forfeit can decide the tournament's historical champion, while the full-fight unlock rules below still require actual completed battles.

## Draws and replays

A full six-round draw does not choose a winner. Kumite issues a fresh room using the same registered gangs when you next check in. The replay retains the existing pairing's round cutoff; it does not restart the deadline.

Completing a draw counts toward participation. That participation counts **once per bracket pairing**, including all draw replays. Replaying a drawn quarterfinal repeatedly cannot produce extra participation credit. A decisive full replay can add the pairing's win credit and, if it is the final, its full-final credit.

## Permanent fighter roadmap

Your roadmap tracks three lifetime totals: completed full Kumite pairings, wins from full battles, and wins from full finals. Progress carries across tournament weeks. Grants happen automatically when a milestone is reached; the roadmap shows your actual progress and whether the milestone is unlocked.

| Fighter | Rarity | Kumite requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bushedo Brown | Rare | Complete 1 full fight |
| Franklin Duke | Epic | Win 1 full fight |
| Chonk Lee | Legendary | Win 1 full final |
| Dragonfly Jones | Legendary | Win 3 full fights |
| Undercova Brotha | Rare | Win 5 full fights |
| Sho'Nuff | Legendary | Win 8 full fights |
| Leroy | Mythical | Win 2 full finals |
| Yasuke | Mythical | Win 3 full finals |

“Full” means the battle reaches its normal six-round completion. Registration alone, byes, no-shows, surrender and turn-timeout results do not add these totals. A full loss or draw can earn Bushedo's participation credit; full wins build the win total; a full final win builds both win and final totals.

Several milestones can unlock together. Winning all three full fights in a filled eight-player bracket reaches the first participation, first win, first full-final and three-win milestones. Advancing through byes does not add the skipped fights to your totals.

Already own a roadmap fighter through an existing route? Completing its Kumite milestone preserves the card's existing XP. Ownership and the roadmap milestone are separate states; seeing that you already own a card does not mean its Kumite requirement was completed. Qualifying recorded progress from earlier Kumite weeks also counts when the roadmap is refreshed.

Kumite's dedicated rewards are fighter cards. There is no additional tournament Clout, ticket, skin or duplicate-shard payout configured. Normal qualifying PvP bounty progress can still apply to a genuine human battle; the tournament does not replace those existing systems.

## Three Kumite-exclusive fighters

Bushedo Brown, Franklin Duke and Chonk Lee are earned through Kumite. They stay out of Street Packs, variant pack pools, Neighborhood Recruit and borrowed draft offers. The other five roadmap fighters retain their existing acquisition routes. Once owned, these cards are usable in ordinary saved gangs that satisfy the usual ownership rules.

## Read the board, history and records

The hanging board shows assigned opponents, check-in state, winners, starts, finishes and actual fight durations. Opening a room is not the fight's start time: timing starts when both players have readied up. Draw replays have their own new battle timing. Unplayed byes and unattended forfeits do not receive invented durations.

**Past Winners** preserves the champion's registered name, hero and avatar, even if that player changes them later. The screen returns the latest 52 champion entries. A legitimate champion decided by a forfeit belongs in that archive.

**Fight Records** is stricter and covers the latest 52 stored weekly tournaments:

- **Fastest fight:** the shortest verified, decisive full battle.
- **Recent winners:** up to ten champions who won full finals.
- **Hand Throwers:** up to ten players ordered by full-fight wins, then full-final titles.

Draws, byes, surrender, timeouts and invalid or missing battle receipts do not enter these records. A forfeit champion can appear in Past Winners while being absent from the full-final recent-winners list. These are recent Kumite records, rather than all-time world records. They are separate from lifetime roadmap totals and Fade Park RP rankings.

## Mobile navigation and the Safehouse shortcut

The Safehouse's new shortcut uses Dragonfly Jones and Chonk Lee in a chibi fight, with a separate live **KUMITE** label. It sits beneath the Mythic shortcut and opens the entrance directly.

Kumite owns its vertical scroll area inside the game screen. The signup panel and banner section controls appear directly below the board, ahead of secondary dates/records flags and the roadmap. Swipe the bracket or flags sideways to read more; continue swiping vertically to reach the final roadmap rewards. After inspecting a fighter and going back, your Kumite scroll position is restored.

The board, hanging cloth and fighting mat retain their artwork proportions across screen sizes. Narrow views crop the scoreboard's outer decoration to keep live names readable. Assigned rooms have their own Kumite VS presentation and separate victory/defeat scenes, with a return to the tournament flow.

## PvP rank-up presentation

Ordinary PvP wins keep the normal result screen. The rank-up cinematic opens only when a ranked result moves you from your previous tier into a **higher tier**. Gaining RP within the same tier, moving down a tier, or receiving previously earned milestone gifts without a current tier promotion does not open that cinematic.

The trigger uses the actual before/after rank, rather than the number of gifts attached to the result. A ranked draw that genuinely crosses into a higher tier can promote too. This presentation fix does not change RP calculations or the settlement of earned rewards. After dismissing the cinematic, the ordinary result and return controls remain available.
