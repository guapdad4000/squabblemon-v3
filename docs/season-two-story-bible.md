# Squabblemon Season Two — Blockbuster on the Block

## Canonical promise

Techbro Rich's rooftop purchase threatens the neighborhood's gathering place the morning after the Crown. The Crown schedules games; it never grants ownership. Matches settle only announced event terms such as stage time, loading routes, speaking order, and access to equipment. The roof is ultimately secured through dated evidence, repairs, fundraising, partner guarantees, a funded cooperative lease, and an enforceable public-use schedule.

## Three-act movement

- **Act I — Disruption (Chapters 9–10):** Rich arrives during cleanup. Conflicting work orders expose his premature launch setup. The block earns time and raises a modest repair fund without confusing a successful party with a purchase.
- **Act II — Work and competing solutions (Chapters 11–14):** Repairs pass inspection; publicity attracts both support and worse buyers; a sourced community exhibit proves sustained use; competing rescue plans reveal their access costs. Blue learns to replace control with transparent, removable responsibility.
- **Act III — Collective action (Chapters 15–16):** Premiere night demonstrates safe operations and completes a documented agreement chain. The cooperative funds and signs a lease with public-use terms while partner venues preserve a wider network.

## Character locks

OG Uncle lives and participates without illness becoming a countdown. Cracked Head's community labor never substitutes for his specific parenting commitments. Baby Momma controls privacy around her child and separates family trust from public praise. Wifey authors governance and repair priorities rather than merely correcting Blue. Blue's growth is behavioral: he publishes terms, reads totals, accepts rotating authority, and does setup work.

The cellblock ensemble contributes purposeful craft, testimony, audio, and program experience. The exact display name is **Regular guy named LeBron James**, an appliance-repair guy whose famous-name misunderstanding drives publicity comedy without making him an athlete.

## Setup and payoff

The conflicting delivery order in Chapter 9 becomes proof that Rich staged a launch before his public promise. The fundraiser pays for actual inspection work. Repair records become the museum's sourced chronology. Publicity brings buyers, forcing the group to compare access terms rather than chase attention. Partner-venue agreements introduced as continuity protection become the guarantee supporting the lease. Rich's inflatable letters physically deflate and leave during the final teardown.

The ending resolves the rooftop threat through action and agreement, not a supernatural or legal effect of winning a card match. A film reel labeled `MISSING MOTION` points toward the theater's next story.
